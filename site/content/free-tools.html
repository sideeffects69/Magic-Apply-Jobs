The three kinds of tool

Search for a LinkedIn auto apply tool and you will find hundreds of names. Underneath, they fall into three groups, and the group tells you more about cost, privacy and effort than the brand does.

  • Subscription services. Web apps that find and apply to jobs for you, often on the provider’s servers, usually with a monthly fee.
  • Browser extensions. Add-ons that work inside your logged-in browser. Many are free to start and charge for more applications.
  • Free open-source programs. Code you run on your own computer, such as Magic Apply - Jobs. You can read exactly what it does.

How they compare

When a free open-source tool is the weaker choice

  • You want a polished hosted service with support. Here, help comes from the community on GitHub.
  • You do not want to run anything on your computer. This tool needs Chrome and works while your computer is on.
  • You need guaranteed compatibility. It is a beta, tested against realistic mock sites, not every employer.
  • You want a different resume written for every job. This tool does not rewrite your resume per job. It can answer screening questions with optional AI, and it can build an ATS-friendly resume from details you enter.

How to choose

  • Decide how much you mind your resume and answers being stored by a third party.
  • Check what a free tier really allows per month, and what it costs once you go past it.
  • Ask whether the tool submits applications or only fills them in for you to send.
  • Read how it handles company websites, since that is where most jobs on LinkedIn end up.
  • Accept that the LinkedIn risk is the same for all of them, and start with a low daily cap.
