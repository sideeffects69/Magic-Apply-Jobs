Short answer: LinkedIn has not published an official number. Many job seekers report being blocked after roughly 50 Easy Apply submissions in 24 hours. Treat that as a report, not a rule, because LinkedIn can change it at any time.

What reaching the limit looks like

When you reach it, LinkedIn shows a message and stops accepting Easy Apply submissions until your count drops. People report three things about it:

  • It works on a rolling 24-hour basis, not a reset at midnight in your time zone.
  • LinkedIn Premium does not raise it.
  • It applies to Easy Apply submissions. Jobs that send you to the employer’s own website are not affected.

These details come from job seekers’ reports, not from LinkedIn. If LinkedIn tells you something different, believe LinkedIn. Its own rules about automation are in its Help Center: Automated activity on LinkedIn.

What to do when you hit it

  1. Stop and wait. The simplest fix is to try again after a full day.
  2. Switch to company sites. Jobs that apply on the employer’s own site are reported not to count towards the limit. See how to apply on company career sites.
  3. Aim better, not more. Fewer applications to jobs that really fit are easier to review and follow up.
  4. Do not try to trick the counter. Working around a limit is likely to look worse to LinkedIn than stopping. Treat it as a signal to slow down.

How Magic Apply - Jobs handles the limit

  • A daily cap of your own. Max applications per day is 40 by default (Run settings), comfortably below the reported 50. The tool counts the applications recorded today and stops when it reaches your cap. Set it to -1 to turn the cap off, or lower it to be more careful.
  • It notices LinkedIn’s limit message. With Apply on company websites on, which is the default, the tool carries on with jobs that apply on company sites and skips Easy Apply jobs for the rest of the run. With it off, the tool stops cleanly and tells you to resume tomorrow.
  • Restore your backup first. The tool forgets everything when you close it, including the list of what you applied for today. Restore your backup at the start of a session so the cap counts your earlier applications.

Using the tool at all carries risk, whatever the cap. Read Is LinkedIn auto apply safe? before you rely on it.

Choosing a daily cap

There is no number that is guaranteed safe. A reasonable approach is to start well below the reported limit, for example 10 to 20 a day for the first few days while you watch how it behaves, then raise it gradually if you are comfortable. Keep Pause before submitting on so that a person reviews each application. Volume is not the goal: a handful of well-matched applications usually beats a hundred that do not fit.
